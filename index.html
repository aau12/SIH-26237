Attribution for multi-recipient encrypted documents

Every copy knows its reader.

When one sealed file goes to a whole group, a leak points at everyone. TraceMark marks each copy at the moment it is opened, makes the reader sign for it, and writes that receipt to a ledger no single administrator can rewrite.

How real is this demo? The invisible text watermark and the SHA-256 hash chain run for real in your browser. You can copy a marked document and paste it back in to trace it. Post-quantum encryption and signatures (ML-KEM-768, ML-DSA-65) are simulated. The people, units and document are fictional samples.

    What runs here, and what doesn't
    
      
      
        
    • Real: the text watermark (a 64-bit mark ID plus a CRC-16 check, hidden as zero-width characters after spaces and repeated across the page), the extraction by majority vote, and the SHA-256 hash chain.
      • 
        
    • Simulated: ML-KEM-768 key wrapping and ML-DSA-65 signatures. Sizes shown (1,088-byte ciphertext, 3,309-byte signature) are the published values in NIST FIPS 203 and FIPS 204.
      • 
        
    • The planned system adds a pixel-level mark for printed or photographed pages and uses Reed–Solomon codes instead of simple repetition.
      • 
        
    • A reader who retypes the text by hand defeats any watermark. The signed receipt still shows who opened the document.
